Snoring and mandibular advancement splints

Snoring is common, and often treated as little more than a household joke, until it begins to affect everyone's sleep. For some people, a dental appliance offers a genuinely helpful option. A custom-made mandibular advancement splint, often simply called a snoring appliance, is worn at night and works by gently easing the lower jaw forward to help keep the airway open.

Snoring & Mandibular Advancement Splints

How a snoring appliance works

During sleep, the muscles of the throat relax, and in some people this narrows the airway and produces the vibration we recognise as snoring. By holding the lower jaw a little forward, a mandibular advancement splint helps maintain a more open airway, which can reduce snoring for some people. Because it is custom-made to fit your mouth, it is designed to be as comfortable and unobtrusive as possible.

Is it right for you?

Dr Teresa Viengkhou offers consultations for these appliances at our practice, and will discuss whether one may be suitable for you. Snoring varies a great deal from person to person, and an appliance is not the right answer for everyone, so a proper conversation about your situation is always the starting point.

Snoring and your wider health

It is worth knowing that snoring can sometimes be associated with broader sleep concerns. For this reason, depending on your circumstances, we may suggest you also speak with your GP or a sleep specialist, so that you have the fullest possible picture of your health. A snoring appliance is best considered as part of that wider conversation rather than in isolation.

Taking the first step

If snoring is affecting your rest or your household, we would be glad to talk it through. A consultation is a relaxed, no-pressure way to understand your options and decide whether an appliance might help.

It is also worth bearing in mind that an appliance is one of several approaches to snoring, and that lifestyle factors can play a part too. During your consultation, we are glad to discuss the bigger picture and what might help in your particular case. Our aim is always to offer honest, practical guidance rather than a one-size-fits-all answer, so that you can make a decision that feels right for you and your household.

Frequently asked questions

How does a snoring appliance reduce snoring?

It gently holds the lower jaw slightly forward during sleep, which helps keep the airway more open. This can reduce snoring for some people, though results vary from person to person.

Is a snoring appliance comfortable to wear?

Because it is custom-made to fit your mouth, it is designed to be as comfortable and unobtrusive as possible. As with anything new, it can take a little time to get used to.

Will an appliance treat sleep apnoea?

Snoring can sometimes be associated with broader sleep concerns such as sleep apnoea, which is a medical matter. We may suggest you also speak with your GP or a sleep specialist so your wider health is properly considered.

How do I know if a snoring appliance is right for me?

The best starting point is a consultation with Dr Teresa Viengkhou, who will discuss your situation and whether an appliance may suit you. It is not the right answer for everyone.

How do I look after a snoring appliance?

We will give you simple cleaning and storage advice when you receive it. Keeping it clean and bringing it to your check-ups helps it stay effective and comfortable.

Should I see my doctor about my snoring too?

Sometimes, yes. Because snoring can be linked to wider sleep and health concerns, we may recommend speaking with your GP or a sleep specialist alongside considering a dental appliance.
