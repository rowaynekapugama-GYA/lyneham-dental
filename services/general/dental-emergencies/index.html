General

Emergency dental care

Dental pain and injuries rarely arrive at a convenient moment, and they can be both distressing and difficult to ignore. Whether you are dealing with sudden pain, a broken or knocked-out tooth, a lost filling or swelling, the sooner we see you, the sooner we can help. We would always encourage you not to wait and hope it settles, getting in touch early often makes for a simpler, more comfortable outcome.

Call us now: 02 6249 1785

When to go to hospital

If you have facial swelling that is affecting your breathing or swallowing, a serious injury to your face or jaw, or bleeding that will not stop, call 000 or go to your nearest hospital emergency department.

Dental Emergencies

What counts as a dental emergency

A dental emergency is any situation causing significant pain, bleeding, swelling or trauma to the teeth or mouth. Common examples include a severe or persistent toothache, a cracked or broken tooth, a tooth that has been knocked out, a lost or dislodged filling or crown, or swelling around a tooth or in the gum. If you are unsure whether your situation is urgent, please call us: we are glad to advise.

How we help

When you come in, your dentist will assess your symptoms and the affected area, and may take a digital radiograph to understand what is happening beneath the surface. Our first priority is to relieve your pain and make you comfortable, before discussing the most appropriate way forward. Sometimes a single visit settles things; in other cases we will outline a plan for any further treatment needed.

In the meantime

While you arrange to see us, simple measures can help. For pain, over-the-counter relief used as directed may ease your discomfort. If a tooth has been knocked out, handle it by the crown rather than the root and keep it moist. For swelling, a cold compress against the cheek can offer some relief. Above all, contact us as soon as you can so we can see you promptly.

Getting in touch

If you are in pain or have experienced a dental emergency, please call us as soon as possible on 02 6249 1785. We will do everything we can to see you quickly and put you at ease.

If your emergency occurs outside our opening hours, please leave a message with your contact details and a brief description of the problem, and we will respond as soon as we are able. Knowing what has happened helps us prepare to see you as quickly as possible when we reopen.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if I am in severe tooth pain?

Call us as soon as you can so we can arrange to see you. In the meantime, over-the-counter pain relief used as directed may help ease your discomfort until your appointment.

My tooth has been knocked out: what should I do?

Handle the tooth by the crown, not the root, and keep it moist. Contact us immediately, as acting quickly gives the best chance of a good outcome.

I have lost a filling or crown: is that an emergency?

It is worth contacting us promptly, especially if there is pain or a sharp edge. We can advise on the best course and arrange to see you.

How quickly can I be seen for a dental emergency?

We prioritise urgent cases and will do our best to see you as soon as possible. Please call early in the day where you can, so we can find you a time.

Should I go to hospital or to a dentist for a dental emergency?

For significant facial swelling, difficulty breathing or swallowing, or a serious injury, seek urgent medical care. For most dental concerns, a dentist is the right place: call us and we will advise.

How can I help prevent dental emergencies?

Regular check-ups help catch problems early, and a custom mouthguard protects against sporting knocks. Good daily habits also reduce the risk of decay-related emergencies.
