General

Tooth extractions

Wherever it is possible, our preference is always to save a natural tooth rather than remove it. If a tooth is damaged, our first course is to explore every reasonable means of restoring it, with a filling, a crown, or another treatment. Removing a tooth is never our starting point, but occasionally it becomes the kindest option for your overall oral health, and when it does, we are here to make the experience as calm and comfortable as possible.

Tooth Extractions

When an extraction may be necessary

There are several reasons a tooth may need to be removed: severe decay or damage beyond repair, advanced gum disease, or a tooth that is causing problems for those around it. In each case, your dentist will examine the tooth, take a digital radiograph to understand the size, shape and position of the roots, and discuss your options fully before anything is decided. You will always be well informed and comfortable with the plan before we proceed.

What to expect on the day

Before an extraction, your dentist will make sure the area is thoroughly numb, so that the procedure itself is comfortable. You may feel some pressure, but it should not be painful. We work gently and steadily, explaining what is happening as we go, and we will give you clear guidance on what to expect afterwards before you leave.

Looking after the area afterwards

Some tenderness and minor swelling are normal in the days following an extraction, and usually settle quickly. We will provide simple aftercare advice, including how to keep the area clean and comfortable while it heals, and we are always available should you have any concerns during your recovery.

Complex cases and replacing a tooth

For more involved extractions, we will refer you to a trusted specialist colleague to ensure you receive the most appropriate care. And where a tooth has been removed, we are glad to discuss options for restoring the gap in due course, so that your bite and the appearance of your smile can be maintained.

We understand, too, that cost and timing are practical considerations, and we are always glad to talk these through before anything is arranged. Our aim is for you to feel fully informed and unhurried, with the time and space to ask any question on your mind.

Tooth Extractions

Frequently asked questions

Will having a tooth removed hurt?

We make sure the area is thoroughly numb beforehand, so you should feel pressure rather than pain during the procedure. Most discomfort afterwards is mild and settles within a few days with simple care.

How long does it take to recover from an extraction?

Many people feel much better within a few days, though this varies depending on the tooth and the individual. We will give you clear aftercare advice to support a smooth recovery.

What should I do after a tooth is taken out?

Follow the aftercare advice we provide, keep the area clean, avoid vigorous rinsing at first, and rest where you can. If you have any concerns as you heal, please contact us.

Can I replace the tooth that was removed?

Yes, there are several ways to restore a gap, and we are happy to discuss the options that suit your situation once the area has healed.

Why might my dentist refer me elsewhere for an extraction?

Some extractions are more complex and are best handled by a specialist colleague. Referring you in these cases ensures you receive the most appropriate, expert care .

Is it really necessary to remove the tooth?

We only ever recommend an extraction when a tooth genuinely cannot be saved or is affecting your wider oral health. Your dentist will always explain why, and discuss any alternatives, before proceeding.
