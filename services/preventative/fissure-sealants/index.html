Fissure sealants

The back teeth carry natural grooves, known as fissures, that help with chewing. They are a perfectly normal feature of every smile, but when they are particularly deep or narrow, they can be difficult to clean thoroughly, even with careful brushing. Food and bacteria can settle into them, which makes these grooves one of the more common places for decay to begin. Fissure sealants offer a simple, preventative way to address this.

Fissure Sealants

What a sealant is

A fissure sealant is a thin protective coating applied over the grooves of a tooth, smoothing the surface so that it is easier to keep clean and harder for decay-causing bacteria to take hold. It is a quiet, preventative measure, quietly working in the background to help a healthy tooth stay that way.

What to expect during treatment

Applying a sealant is quick and comfortable, and involves no drilling and no anaesthetic. Your dentist will first clean and prepare the tooth, then carefully apply the sealant material and set it in place. The whole process takes only a few minutes per tooth, which makes it especially well suited to children, though adults with deep grooves can benefit too.

Who benefits most

Sealing teeth early, particularly in childhood, soon after the adult back teeth come through, offers the greatest protective benefit, as it helps prevent decay before it ever has a chance to start. That said, sealants are not only for children: adults whose back teeth have deep, hard-to-clean grooves may also find them worthwhile. Your dentist will assess whether sealants are appropriate for you or your child as part of a routine examination.

Sealants as part of preventative care

It is worth remembering that a sealant is one helpful tool among several, rather than a substitute for good everyday habits. Regular brushing, daily flossing, a sensible diet and routine check-ups all remain essential. Used alongside these, sealants can be a sensible and cost-effective way to help protect vulnerable teeth for the long term.

Many parents are pleasantly surprised by how quick and uneventful the appointment is: there is no need to numb the area, and most children take it entirely in their stride. If you are weighing up whether sealants are worthwhile for your family, we are always glad to talk it through and explain what we would recommend, and why.

Frequently asked questions

Are fissure sealants only for children?

No. While they are most commonly applied to children's adult back teeth soon after they come through, adults with deep or hard-to-clean grooves can benefit too. Your dentist will advise whether they would help you.

Does having a sealant applied hurt?

Not at all. The process involves no drilling and no anaesthetic. Your dentist simply cleans the tooth and applies the protective coating, which takes only a few minutes.

How long do fissure sealants last?

Sealants can last for several years, though they do wear over time. We check them at your regular visits and can reapply or top them up if needed.

Do sealants mean my child won't get cavities?

Sealants significantly help protect the grooves of the back teeth, but they are not a guarantee against decay and do not protect other surfaces. Good brushing, flossing, a sensible diet and regular check-ups all remain important.

Can sealants be placed over an existing cavity?

Sealants are a preventative measure for healthy teeth. If a tooth already has decay, your dentist will discuss the most appropriate treatment with you instead.

How much do fissure sealants cost?

The cost depends on how many teeth are involved. We are always happy to discuss this with you beforehand, and eligible children may be able to access sealants through the Child Dental Benefits Schedule.
