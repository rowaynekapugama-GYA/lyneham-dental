Inlays and onlays

Inlays and onlays occupy the middle ground between a filling and a crown. When a tooth is too damaged for a simple filling yet does not require a full crown, these custom-made restorations rebuild it beautifully, restoring strength and function while preserving as much of your natural tooth as possible. They are a considered, conservative way to give a tooth the support it needs.

Inlays & Onlays

The difference between an inlay and an onlay

The two are closely related. An inlay sits within the central part of a tooth, filling the area between its raised edges, while an onlay extends further to restore one or more of those raised points, or cusps. In both cases, the restoration is crafted to fit your tooth precisely, then bonded securely in place. Your dentist will advise which is appropriate based on the extent and position of the damage.

Why choose an inlay or onlay

Where a tooth has more damage than a filling can comfortably address, an inlay or onlay restores it without the need to reduce the tooth as much as a crown would require. This conservative approach helps preserve more of your natural tooth structure, while providing a strong, durable and natural-looking result. For many teeth, it offers the best of both worlds.

Crafted with modern technology

We craft inlays and onlays in ceramic using our CEREC technology, which allows us to design and create a precise, custom restoration and bond it securely to your tooth. The result looks and feels entirely natural , matched in shade to your surrounding teeth. Your dentist will assess your tooth and advise honestly whether an inlay or onlay is the right choice for you.

Caring for your restoration

An inlay or onlay is cared for just like a natural tooth: regular brushing, daily flossing and routine check-ups will help it last. With sensible care, these restorations are strong and long-lasting, and we will keep an eye on yours at each visit to make sure it continues to serve you well.

If you have been told that a tooth needs more than a filling, an inlay or onlay may well be worth discussing. We are always happy to explain why we are recommending a particular approach, and to answer any questions you have, so that you can make an informed decision about your care with complete confidence.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an inlay and an onlay?

An inlay fits within the central part of a tooth, while an onlay extends to restore one or more of its raised points, or cusps. Both are custom-made and bonded securely in place.

When would I need an inlay or onlay instead of a filling?

When a tooth has more damage than a simple filling can comfortably restore, but does not require a full crown, an inlay or onlay is often the ideal middle-ground option.

Are inlays and onlays strong?

Yes, crafted in ceramic and bonded securely to the tooth, they are strong, durable and designed to withstand normal chewing. With good care they can last many years.

Do inlays and onlays look natural?

They do: we match them in shade to your surrounding teeth, so the restoration blends naturally with your smile.

Does the treatment hurt?

We make sure the tooth is comfortably numb during treatment, so it should not hurt. Some mild sensitivity afterwards is normal and usually settles.

How do I care for an inlay or onlay?

Care for it as you would a natural tooth, with regular brushing, daily flossing and routine check-ups. We will monitor it at your visits to ensure it stays in good condition.
